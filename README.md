# study-of-tiger-poop — A field study of tiger scat
A practical, professional — and slightly cheeky — repository for documenting, identifying, and learning from tiger feces (scat, droppings) for educational and biological field-study purposes. This resource is intended for researchers, students, park staff, and citizen scientists interested in non-invasive monitoring of tiger ecology.

---

## Overview
This repository collects field notes, photographic records, morphology guides, and best practices for the identification and safe handling of tiger scat. The project emphasizes:
- Non-invasive methods for ecological monitoring
- Reproducible field metadata (date, time, GPS, habitat)
- Responsible sampling and chain-of-custody for laboratory analysis (DNA, hormones, parasites)
- Educational materials that make fecal ecology approachable and scientifically rigorous

Whether you are learning to tell tiger scat from other carnivores, preparing samples for lab work, or building a dataset for automated image recognition, this repo is a starting point.

---

## Why it matters
Tiger scat is a small package with a big story:
- **Diet & trophic links:** Scat contains hair, bone fragments, and plant material that reveal recent prey and feeding behaviors.
- **Population & health monitoring:** DNA from feces enables non-invasive genotyping, population estimates, and stress/hormone profiling.
- **Disease & parasite surveillance:** Parasites and pathogens detected in feces inform wildlife health and potential spillover risks.
- **Conservation & management:** Scat surveys complement camera traps and tracks to map territoriality, movement corridors, and human–wildlife interfaces.

Non-invasive sampling is cost-effective and minimizes disturbance to sensitive species while yielding robust ecological data.

## Morphology notes
Use these morphological features to assess and preliminarily identify tiger scat in the field:

- **Size & shape:** Typically large (length 6–30+ cm), tubular or segmented; often tapered ends. Compare against local carnivores (leopards, hyenas).
- **Cross-section & consistency:** Can range from firm to loose depending on diet; moist, glistening scat often indicates recent deposition.
- **Color & age:** Darker when fresh; dries and lightens with exposure. UV or wetting can reveal undigested material.
- **Contents:** Hair and bone fragments are common. Long, coarse hair of ungulate prey is frequently visible; feathers indicate bird predation.
- **Surface marks & additives:** Territorial markings sometimes contain scentiferous secretions or urine; presence of marks from dragging or bedding can indicate behavior.
- **Odor:** Strong, pungent smell when fresh; not diagnostic but helpful for field assessment.
- **Measurement tips:** Always photograph with a scale (ruler, coin) and include a north arrow or landmarks. Record GPS coordinates, elevation, habitat type, and observer.

Note: Morphology is suggestive, not definitive. Combine with genetics, camera trap data, and expert consultation.

## Habitat / Diet clues
Scat location and contents help reconstruct feeding ecology and habitat use:

- **Proximal habitat:** Scat found along trails, ridgelines, water sources, or near kill sites. Multiple fresh deposits in an area can indicate a travel corridor or scent-marking site.
- **Prey identification:** Visible hair, bone fragments, hooves point to ungulate prey (deer, boar); feather fragments or small bones indicate smaller prey. Countable prey remains can estimate meal size.
- **Seasonal signals:** Plant material or fruits in scat may indicate seasonal shifts in diet or opportunistic feeding.
- **Human-related cues:** Presence of domestic animal remains, human refuse, or anthropogenic materials flags potential human–wildlife conflict zones.

Combine scat analysis with habitat mapping and prey surveys for robust ecological inference.

## Ethics & Safety
This work must be done with strict ethical and safety considerations in mind.

- **Permits & permissions:** Always operate under relevant research permits and land-management permissions. Follow local and national wildlife regulations.
- **Non-disturbance:** Do not approach or disturb live tigers or other wildlife to collect scat. Maintain safe distances and follow established park protocols.
- **PPE & hygiene:** Use disposable gloves, masks, and dedicated tools. Avoid direct contact. Change gloves between samples. Sanitize tools and surfaces with appropriate disinfectant.
- **Sample handling:** Use sterile tubes, label clearly with sample ID and metadata, keep cold chain for molecular work, and follow institutional biosafety procedures for transport and storage.
- **Zoonotic risk:** Feces can transmit bacteria, parasites, and viruses. Ensure field teams are trained in biohazard handling and have post-exposure plans.
- **Community engagement:** Inform and involve local communities; respect cultural sensitivities around wildlife and waste. Use findings to support conservation that benefits both tigers and people.
- **Data sensitivity:** Precise GPS locations of tigers are sensitive. Share location data only with authorized personnel and follow data-sharing agreements to reduce poaching risk.

Ethical conduct safeguards both human participants and wildlife populations.

## Future work
Opportunities to expand and professionalize this resource:

- Build a standardized metadata schema (JSON/YAML) for scat records and integrate with existing biodiversity standards (Darwin Core).
- Create an annotated image dataset for machine learning to automate scat identification and freshness classification.
- Develop field-friendly kits and SOPs for safe sample collection, preservation (FTA cards, ethanol), and cold-chain logistics.
- Pilot multidisciplinary studies: diet (metabarcoding), stress (corticosteroids), parasite surveys, and microbiome analyses.
- Launch a controlled citizen-science program with training modules, validation workflows, and secure data-sharing policies.
- Publish protocols and findings to support evidence-based management decisions and anti-poaching strategies.

Contributions, issues, and pull requests are welcome. Please follow contribution guidelines (see CONTRIBUTING.md in this repo) and code of conduct.
